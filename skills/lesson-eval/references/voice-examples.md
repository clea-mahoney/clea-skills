# Voice examples

Replace these with before/after pairs from your own lessons. They define your house voice better than any rule list.

Rules tell you what not to write. These pairs show what the finished sentence looks like. When a draft passes every check and still sounds wrong, it failed here.

Mark pairs that came from a human editing a real draft as `(hand-edited)`. They outrank the others.

---

## Opening a lesson

**Weak:** In this lesson, you will learn how to add an approval step to a workflow.
**Better:** Some workflows touch things you can't undo, like disabling a user account. Before those run, you can make `[COMPANY]`'s product wait for a person to approve.

Why: the opening puts the learner in a situation they recognize, then names the action.

---

## Stating what something is

**Weak:** An approval step isn't a delay, it's a safety net.
**Better:** An approval step pauses the workflow until someone on the approver list clicks Approve.

Why: no negation setting up a positive. One plain statement the learner can act on.

---

## Naming a gap or limitation

**Weak:** Right now, nothing stops a bad run. No check, no pause, no second pair of eyes.
**Better:** Once you add an approval step, the workflow waits for a person before it runs the risky action.

Why: lead with the working state. Name the gap once, after, only if it still helps.

---

## Flagging something important

**Weak:** The part that matters most here is that the approver list controls who can release the run.
**Better:** The approver list controls who can release the run.

Why: a sentence that needs a label to be important isn't.

---

## Cutting stock phrases *(hand-edited)*

**Weak:** The first call is which workflow to protect first.
**Better:** Start with the workflow that would hurt most if it ran on the wrong data.

Why: "first call" is a stock phrase. Say the literal thing.
